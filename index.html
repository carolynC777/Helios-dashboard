Project overview

How does a changing Sun affect our orbital environment?

This project compares near-Earth solar-wind observations across 2018–2021 and 2022–2025, explores solar activity, and examines documented satellite impacts.

Main finding: Median wind speed increased by 43 km/s between periods. This comparison describes the selected years; it does not establish a long-term trend or prove an increase in satellite failures.

My contribution & project development

Carolyn Cabrales defined the research topic, comparison questions and interactive features, and directed the dashboard's design and revisions. AI assistance was used to retrieve and summarize public data, implement the interface and calculations, and check controls. Sources, missing-value handling and model assumptions are documented below.

Next research step: expand the incident evidence and examine Bz and geomagnetic activity alongside wind speed before drawing conclusions about satellite-disruption risk.

Methods & provenance

Read this first: 2018–2025 only; 2026, population-wide outage counts, forecasts and measured atmospheric time series are excluded. The timeline provides selected documented cases. Scenario calculations are hypothetical. NASA fill codes are omitted per field, with no imputation. Each period has 35,064 expected hours and ≥34,572 valid wind-speed hours. The 600 km/s cutoff is descriptive, not a satellite-risk threshold. Hover, focus or tap marks for details.

Sources: NASA SPDF OMNI2 annual hourly files · Field definitions · NOAA observed sunspot data (ssn) · Storm mechanisms · Snapshot retrieved October 31, 2026 · Download chart data (JSON)
